## 1. Which daily-data features have been shown to identify manipulation?

### Takeaway
Across SEC, Taiwan, Australian/Canadian, Thai, Indian and Indonesian evidence, the same daily signature keeps appearing. Manipulated stocks are small, illiquid and often poorly governed. During the manipulation they show abnormal positive returns, abnormal volume/turnover and higher volatility and liquidity. Prices then partly or fully reverse afterwards. For closing-price manipulation, the signature is a large day-end move that reverses the next morning. No study validates a single feature on its own. The strongest base set is (a) market-adjusted abnormal return and cumulative run-up, (b) abnormal volume/value/turnover relative to the stock's own history, (c) volatility, (d) reversal after the run-up, and (e) cross-sectional "vulnerability" priors (small cap, low free float, high Amihud illiquidity).

### Cited Findings
- **SEC enforcement cases (US):** Manipulations are associated with higher stock volatility, liquidity and returns. Prices and liquidity are higher when the manipulator sells than when they buy. Manipulated stocks tend to be small and illiquid. — [Aggarwal & Wu (2006), *Journal of Business* 79(4):1915-1954, RePEc record](https://econpapers.repec.org/RePEc:ucp:jnlbus:v:79:y:2006:i:4:p:1915-1954); [author PDF](https://www.bauer.uh.edu/wu/Papers/JOB2006_StockManipu.pdf)
- **Taiwan, pump-and-dump vs stabilisation:** Pump-and-dump cases show large temporary price spikes, higher volatility, heavy volume, short-term price continuation and long-term reversals. Stabilisation cases had little market effect. Manipulated firms tend to be small with weak corporate governance. Damage to efficiency was greater when the firm's fundamentals were negative. — [Huang & Cheng (2015), "Stock manipulation and its effects: pump and dump versus stabilization," *Review of Quantitative Finance and Accounting* 44(4):791-815](https://ideas.repec.org/a/kap/rqfnac/v44y2015i4p791-815.html)
- An earlier Taiwan study (Huang, Chen & Cheng 2008) used hand-collected Taiwan SEC cases from 1991-2005 and reached similar conclusions: manipulated stocks were smaller and prices reversed. — [summary via search; record](https://www.indianjournalofentrepreneurship.com/index.php/IJF/article/view/71636) (citation details are from the search summary and should be verified)
- **Closing-price manipulation:** Comerton-Forde & Putniņš used prosecuted closing-price manipulation cases from US and Canadian exchanges (1997-2009). They quantified the effects on trading characteristics and price accuracy, and turned the results into an index of the probability and intensity of closing-price manipulation. The index is intended for markets and periods without prosecution data. — [Comerton-Forde & Putniņš (2011), "Measuring closing price manipulation," *Journal of Financial Intermediation* 20(2):135-158](https://ideas.repec.org/a/eee/jfinin/v20y2011i2p135-158.html); [UTS repository](https://opus.cloud1.lib.uts.edu.au/handle/10453/31358)
- **Variables in that index [prior knowledge, not verified in-session]:** day-end return (last ~30 min), next-day return reversal, the bid-ask spread at the close, and trading activity in the final minutes, each measured relative to the stock's own normal level. Search results did *not* confirm the variable list, so it should be checked against the published paper or the [Putniņš thesis (core.ac.uk)](https://core.ac.uk/download/41234083.pdf).
- **Who gets manipulated, and how much goes undetected:** About 1% of closing prices are manipulated, and only a small fraction of these are detected and prosecuted. Manipulation is most likely in stocks with high information asymmetry and mid-to-low liquidity. — [Comerton-Forde & Putniņš (2014), "Stock Price Manipulation: Prevalence and Determinants," *Review of Finance* 18(1):23-66](https://econpapers.repec.org/RePEc:oup:revfin:v:18:y:2014:i:1:p:23-66.); the thesis version estimates ~1.1% of closes and ~300 undetected or unprosecuted cases for every prosecuted one — [Putniņš thesis via search summary](https://core.ac.uk/download/41234083.pdf)
- Related classic closing-price literature: [Hillion & Suominen (2004), "The manipulation of closing prices," *Journal of Financial Markets* 7(4):351-375](https://ideas.repec.org/a/eee/jfinin/v20y2011i2p135-158.html) (cited in C-F&P's reference list); Harris (1989), "A Day-End Transaction Price Anomaly," *JFQA* 24(1):29-45 (same source).
- **Indonesia:** A study of 149 IDX firms over one month in late 2015 used the Allen & Gorton (1992) framework. It found manipulation in 14 periods and a positive correlation between trading volume and cumulative abnormal return (CAR) over the following four trading days. It also examined market cap and price volatility. — ["Pump-Dump Manipulation Analysis: The Influence of Market Capitalization and Its Impact on Stock Price Volatility at Indonesia Stock Exchange" (ResearchGate)](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/344164378_Pump-Dump_Manipulation_Analysis_The_Influence_of_Market_Capitalization_and_Its_Impact_on_Stock_Price_Volatility_at_Indonesia_Stock_Exchange)
- **Indonesia (thesis):** A UGM undergraduate thesis analysed DSSA from January 2021 to April 2026 using the Amihud illiquidity ratio, abnormal return, relative valuation and DCF-FCFF as "saham gorengan" indicators. This is a thesis, not peer-reviewed. — [UGM ETD record](https://etd.repository.ugm.ac.id/penelitian/detail/278585)
- **Indonesian practitioner and press characterisations of saham gorengan:** small market cap; low liquidity; unusually high volume or value in small caps; appearance on the UMA list. — [Bareksa](https://www.bareksa.com/berita/saham/2024-01-25/jangan-terjebak-saham-gorengan-ini-ciri-ciri-dan-cara-menghindarinya/amp); [Suara.com (Dec 2025)](https://www.suara.com/bisnis/2025/12/10/153059/ribut-saham-gorengan) (press, not peer-reviewed)
- **India:** Before bulk deals, trading volume and delivery percentage rise significantly. Lagged CAR and changes in volume and delivery explain event-day abnormal returns. — [IIM Calcutta WP 863](https://studentlive.iimcal.ac.in/sites/all/files/pdfs/wp_863.pdf) (working paper). NSE's ASM surveillance flags securities with "abnormal price and traded volume variations". — [NSE working paper on ASM effectiveness](https://nsearchives.nseindia.com/s3fs-public/inline-files/Effectiveness%20of%20additional%20surveillance%20measures_WorkingPaper.pdf)
- **Crypto pump-and-dumps** are detected from abnormal price *and* volume spikes against recent history. — [Kamps & Kleinberg (2018), "To the moon: defining and detecting cryptocurrency pump-and-dumps," *Crime Science* 7:18](https://crimesciencejournal.biomedcentral.com/track/pdf/10.1186/s40163-018-0093-5); [BMC blog summary](https://blogs.biomedcentral.com/on-society/2019/01/22/cryptocurrency-pump-and-dumps/)
- **Amihud illiquidity** (|r| / value traded, averaged): [Amihud (2002), *Journal of Financial Markets* 5(1):31-56, doi:10.1016/S1386-4181(01)00024-6](https://doi.org/10.1016/S1386-4181(01)00024-6) **[prior knowledge, not verified in-session]**

### Inferences
- **Candidate daily feature set** for an engineer, grouped by mechanism. Each feature should be computed per stock-day using only past data:
  1. *Abnormal return:* r_t minus beta times r_market (IHSG/JCI or a size-matched index), plus cumulative AR over 5, 10, 20 and 60 days. Also a run-up measure: max drawdown-reversed cumulative return.
  2. *Abnormal activity:* log(volume), log(value) and log(trade frequency), each as a robust z-score against the stock's own trailing 60-120-day median/MAD. Also turnover (volume / free-float shares) and its abnormal version.
  3. *Volatility:* Parkinson or Garman-Klass range volatility from OHLC, its ratio to the trailing level, and absolute-return z-scores.
  4. *Price-volume coupling:* rolling correlation of |r| or r with abnormal volume. Positive same-day and lead coupling matches the Indonesian volume-to-CAR finding.
  5. *Reversal or "dump":* post-peak drawdown, and the ratio of current price to its 20/60-day max. Useful for labelling and retrospective scoring. Online, only the partial reversal observed so far is usable.
  6. *Limit and streak features:* number of ARA (upper-limit) closes in the last N days, consecutive ARA streaks, and ARB hits after ARA streaks (see Section 5).
  7. *Closing-price proxies (EOD-only):* close relative to the day's range, (C − L)/(H − L); close vs VWAP (value / volume), where a close well above VWAP suggests marking the close; next-day open-to-prior-close reversal; month-end and quarter-end flags.
  8. *Trade-size and frequency structure:* average trade size = value / frequency. Very small average trades with very high frequency may indicate fragmented or wash activity. Abnormal frequency without abnormal value is another flag.
  9. *Foreign flow:* net foreign buy/sell as a share of value. Domestic-only surges in small caps were a pattern noted by an Indonesian broker study (Section 3).
  10. *Static vulnerability priors:* market cap, free-float percentage, price level (penny stocks below Rp 200, or under Rp 51 for the special board), Amihud, share of zero-volume days, listing age, and Special Monitoring Board / notation status.
- Because manipulated stocks are systematically small and illiquid, raw anomaly scores will be dominated by thin stocks. Normalising within liquidity/size buckets (or cross-sectional ranking per day) is likely required.

### Gaps
- The exact variable list and coefficients of the Comerton-Forde & Putniņš index could not be verified (fetches blocked). The index also relies on intraday data. Its EOD transfer, via close-vs-VWAP and next-open reversal proxies, is the researcher's inference and was not validated in any source found.
- No source was found that formally tests trade-frequency/trade-size ratios or foreign-flow features for manipulation detection on IDX.

## 2. Detection approaches and what comparative studies find

### Takeaway
The literature has four families: (1) threshold or rule screens (exchange surveillance, Kamps & Kleinberg-style spike rules); (2) statistical anomaly scores and event studies; (3) unsupervised ML trained on "normal" data (autoencoders, GANs, isolation forest); (4) supervised or semi-supervised classifiers trained on prosecuted cases. On small labelled sets, simple models do competitively. Naive Bayes beat SVM, CART, KNN and Random Forest on SEC cases. The Thai line of work evaluates on only around six prosecuted cases, and ensembles of supervised and unsupervised models did best there. No published study applying PU learning directly to trade-based stock manipulation was found. PU learning has been applied to Chinese A-share accounting fraud, a close analogue.

### Cited Findings
- **Supervised, SEC cases:** Golmohammadi, Zaïane & Díaz compared Naive Bayes, SVM, CART, KNN and Random Forest on the Diaz et al. dataset of SEC-reported 2003 manipulation cases. Naive Bayes did best: F2 = 53%, sensitivity 89%, specificity 83%. Data granularity (daily vs transaction-level) was not confirmed from the search summary. — [Golmohammadi, Zaïane & Díaz (2014), "Detecting Stock Market Manipulation using Supervised Learning Algorithms," IEEE DSAA 2014, pp. 435-441, doi:10.1109/DSAA.2014.7058109](https://ieeexplore.ieee.org/document/7058109/); [PDF](https://webdocs.cs.ualberta.ca/~zaiane/postscript/DSAA2014.pdf)
- A follow-up unsupervised paper argues that supervised methods are limited by the dearth of labelled data and by their inability to recognise novel tactics beyond those labelled. Labels are rare because they are costly and need investigation. — [search summary pointing to Zaïane group work, e.g. DaWaK 2017 PDF](https://webdocs.cs.ualberta.ca/~zaiane/postscript/dawak17.pdf)
- **Thailand (SET), a progression of methods on very few prosecuted cases:**
  - Feedforward NN on level-1 data: 88.28% on pump-and-dump; failed on spoofing (2016). — [KMUTT record](https://kirim.kmutt.ac.th/converis/portal/detail/Publication/601033060?lang=de_DE)
  - GAN with LSTM trained only on normal trading: 68.1% accuracy on unseen pump-and-dump (IEEE SSCI 2018). — [KMUTT record](https://kirim.kmutt.ac.th/converis/portal/detail/Publication/601505656?lang=en_GB); [doi:10.1109/SSCI.2018.8628777](https://unpaywall.org/10.1109%2FSSCI.2018.8628777)
  - Deep unsupervised learning (autoencoder, GAN) on normal order-book behaviour: identified 5 of 6 prosecuted cases with a low false-positive rate (IEEE Access 2021). — [Leangarun et al., "Stock Price Manipulation Detection Using Deep Unsupervised Learning: The Case of Thailand" (NSTDA PDF)](https://nstda.or.th/openarchive/wp-content/uploads/tainacan-items/2494/99565/Stock_Price_Manipulation_Detection_Using_Deep_Unsupervised_Learning_The_Case_of_Thailand.pdf)
  - Transformer trained on *synthesised* pump-and-dump patterns: 5 of 6 real SEC-prosecuted cases detected, low false positives (Chullamonthon & Tangamchit 2022). — [KMUTT record](https://kirim.kmutt.ac.th/converis/portal/detail/Publication/986842852?lang=en_GB)
  - Ensemble of LSTM classifier, LSTM autoencoder and SVM stacker: 6 of 6 cases with low false-alarm rates (*Expert Systems with Applications* 2023). — [KMUTT record](https://kirim.kmutt.ac.th/converis/portal/detail/Publication/1366062027?lang=en_GB)
  - A later student capstone added SHAP explainability to the LSTM detector (not peer-reviewed). — [KMUTT capstone PDF](https://inc.kmutt.ac.th/download/capstone_design_projects/2567/4.pdf)
  - Caveat: figures come from abstracts and use different data, labels and setups, so they are not directly comparable. — (same sources)
- **Crypto:** Kamps & Kleinberg treat pump-and-dumps as anomalies and flag unusual price and volume spikes, evaluating on a small set of manually identified pumps. — [Kamps & Kleinberg (2018), *Crime Science*](https://discovery.ucl.ac.uk/id/eprint/10069142/). La Morgia et al. report a real-time classifier that beats Kamps & Kleinberg and cuts expected detection time from 30 minutes to 25 seconds (**preprint/arXiv version**; published as conference/journal papers by the same group). — [La Morgia, Mei, Sassi & Stefa, "The Doge of Wall Street: Analysis and Detection of Pump and Dump Cryptocurrency Manipulations," arXiv:2105.00733](https://arxiv.org/html/2105.00733v2)
- **China:** A 2019 *Neurocomputing* paper built a labelled dataset from CSRC-prosecuted cases with RNN plus ensemble learning. It reported a 29.8% average AUC gain over prior methods (intraday). — [record via search](https://bohrium.dp.tech/paper/arxiv/af6a1ac28195b8d201c68ab34a138fefce5f0fc67083fd8402b8a50278551cb2). An *International Review of Financial Analysis* 2021 paper applied ML (e.g. SVM) to CSRC punishment cases from 2014-2016 together with firm financial data. — [IDEAS record](https://ideas.repec.org/a/eee/finana/v78y2021ics1057521921002143.html). A 2024 Emerald (*Kybernetes*) study combined a jump test with a DNN and noted that publicly known cases are few. — [Emerald](https://www.emerald.com/insight/content/doi/10.1108/k-01-2024-0188/full/html). A Hong Kong student project (not peer-reviewed) reported supervised ML working well on *daily* data but poorly on tick data. — [HKU FYP interim report](https://wp2023.cs.hku.hk/fyp23058/wp-content/uploads/sites/59/FYP-Interim-Report-1.pdf)
- **Isolation forest variant:** A 2024 *Mathematics* paper uses k-partitioned isolation forests for manipulation detection. — [IDEAS record, *Mathematics* 12(9):1336](https://ideas.repec.org/a/gam/jmathe/v12y2024i9p1336-d1384579.html)
- **PU learning:** Applied to Chinese A-share *accounting* fraud (2001-2022 data), where it "significantly improves" detection. Firm-level fraud, not trade-based manipulation. — [Xi'an Jiaotong Univ. record](https://scholar.xjtu.edu.cn/en/publications/detecting-accounting-fraud-in-china-a-share-market-with-pu-learni/). A "PU-Detector" journal paper frames a financial transaction-type detection task as PU (only positives labelled). The transaction type could not be confirmed. — [ACM doi:10.1145/3638561](https://unpaywall.org/10.1145%2F3638561)
- **PU theory [prior knowledge, not verified in-session]:** Elkan & Noto (2008) show that under "selected completely at random" (SCAR), a classifier separating labelled from unlabelled examples is proportional to the true posterior, with the constant c = P(labelled | positive). — [doi:10.1145/1401890.1401920](https://doi.org/10.1145/1401890.1401920). Survey: Bekker & Davis (2020), "Learning from positive and unlabeled data: a survey," *Machine Learning* 109:719-760 — [doi:10.1007/s10994-020-05877-5](https://doi.org/10.1007/s10994-020-05877-5).
- **Isolation forest [prior knowledge, not verified in-session]:** Liu, Ting & Zhou (2008), ICDM — [doi:10.1109/ICDM.2008.17](https://doi.org/10.1109/ICDM.2008.17). **Change-point detection (PELT)** [prior knowledge]: Killick, Fearnhead & Eckley (2012), *JASA* 107:1590-1598 — [doi:10.1080/01621459.2012.737745](https://doi.org/10.1080/01621459.2012.737745).
- **Regulator rule screens:** NSE India monitors trade data in real time and flags securities with abnormal price and traded-volume variations for ASM/STASM. Event studies show CAR decreasing and abnormal volume changing after inclusion. — [NSE-NYU Stern white paper](https://nsearchives.nseindia.com/s3fs-public/inline-files/Effectiveness%20of%20additional%20surveillance%20measures_WhitePaper.pdf); [Chari & Inamdar (2024), *Review of Pacific Basin Financial Markets & Policies* 27(1)](https://ideas.repec.org/a/wsi/rpbfmp/v27y2024i01ns0219091524500048.html)
- **IDX:** IDX says it will use surveillance technology for early detection of price manipulation and will restrict or suspend securities accounts during examinations. It also notes that UMA and suspension alone are not enough. — [JawaPos](https://www.jawapos.com/ekonomi/2610260168/bei-perketat-pengawasan-saham-gorengan-tak-cukup-uma-dan-suspensi) (press)

### Inferences
- **Recommended layered design** for ~13 positive windows:
  1. **Transparent rule/z-score screen** (robust MAD z-scores of abnormal return, volume, value and frequency; ARA streaks), used as a baseline and as features.
  2. **Unsupervised anomaly model** (isolation forest on the feature vector, per liquidity bucket). This follows the Thai and Zaïane conclusion that models trained on normal data generalise to unseen tactics.
  3. **PU or weakly supervised ranker** (logistic regression or gradient boosting with strong regularisation). Positive = days inside OJK windows; unlabelled = everything else, with UMA and suspension as noisy auxiliary positives. Use Elkan-Noto calibration or nnPU. SCAR is clearly violated: regulators prosecute big, visible cases. Treat output as a ranking, not a calibrated probability.
  4. **Ensemble or stacking**, as in the Thai 2023 result.
- **Event-level structure:** run change-point detection (PELT or Bayesian online CPD) on abnormal turnover and cumulative AR to segment candidate "episodes". Then score episodes rather than isolated days. This fits month-granularity labels better (Section 4).
- **Synthetic positives:** the Thai Transformer trained on synthesised pump-and-dump patterns. Injecting synthetic run-up / ARA-streak / reversal patterns into normal histories could augment 13 real cases. The risk is that the model learns the generator.
- **Sequence models (LSTM, Transformer)** need far more positives than 13. They are best kept as unsupervised reconstruction models (autoencoders) or deferred.
- **Broker-level add-on (later):** broker summaries (top buyer/seller concentration, the same broker on both sides, broker churn) would enable wash-trade and coordinated-trading detection. A daily OHLCV feed cannot distinguish these from genuine demand. An Indonesian study already used broker behaviour to find pump-and-dump cycles (Section 3).

### Gaps
- No head-to-head comparison of isolation forest vs autoencoder vs PU vs rules on *daily* equity data with prosecuted labels was found.
- Full methods for Golmohammadi et al. (granularity, feature list) and for the IRFA 2021 paper could not be fetched.
- No published PU-learning application to trade-based stock manipulation was found. This appears to be a genuine literature gap.

## 3. Key papers, including emerging-market and Indonesian studies

### Takeaway
The core canon is Allen & Gale (1992) and Allen & Gorton (1992) for theory, Aggarwal & Wu (2006) for SEC-case empirics, Comerton-Forde & Putniņš (2011, 2014) for closing-price measurement and prevalence, and Huang & Cheng (2015) for Taiwan. Detection work includes Golmohammadi et al. (2014), the Thai Leangarun/KMUTT series, and the crypto work of Kamps & Kleinberg (2018) and La Morgia et al. Indonesian peer-reviewed detection work is thin. It consists mainly of event studies of IPO pump-and-dump, an Allen-Gorton-based volume/CAR study, a broker-behaviour study and legal analyses (TAXI, LUCK cases).

Shows that trade-based manipulation (pure buying and selling, without false information or actions) can be profitable when investors cannot tell an informed trader from a manipulator. — [doi:10.1093/rfs/5.3.503](https://doi.org/10.1093/rfs/5.3.503) **[prior knowledge, not verified in-session]**

- **India:** Chari & Inamdar (2024) on the effects of STASM placement; NSE-NYU white paper on ASM; IIM Calcutta WP on bulk deals. — links in Section 1/2.

### Inferences
- The Indonesian literature confirms the *same* daily signature as the international literature: volume leads CAR, small caps, broker-driven run-ups. It offers no validated detector. The project would be among the first systematic daily IDX detectors, so the write-up should frame results as exploratory.
- IPO windows are a known IDX pump-and-dump locus, so listing age should be both a feature and a stratifier.

### Gaps
- No peer-reviewed Sinta/Garuda paper was found that builds or evaluates a *detector* using UMA notices or OJK sanctions as labels. A deeper Garuda search was not possible within the tool budget.
- The FCA (UK), SEC and IOSCO surveillance documentation was not retrieved in this pass.

## 4. Evaluation with sparse, noisy, coarse labels

### Takeaway
With ~13 confirmed windows, day-level classification metrics are noisy and misleading. Evaluation should be **event-level**: did the detector rank the stock in the daily top-k at least once inside or shortly before the window? It should also report **lead time**, **precision@k against weak labels**, and the **daily alert budget**. All of this should run in a strict walk-forward setup with point-in-time features. The literature on very few cases (Thai: 5/6 and 6/6 cases detected "with low false-positive rate") effectively uses event-level recall plus a false-alarm rate, which is the right template.

### Cited Findings
- Thai studies report results as cases detected out of prosecuted cases (5/6, 6/6) together with a "low false-positive rate". This is event-level recall with an FPR constraint. — [NSTDA PDF](https://nstda.or.th/openarchive/wp-content/uploads/tainacan-items/2494/99565/Stock_Price_Manipulation_Detection_Using_Deep_Unsupervised_Learning_The_Case_of_Thailand.pdf); [KMUTT ESWA record](https://kirim.kmutt.ac.th/converis/portal/detail/Publication/1366062027?lang=en_GB)
- Golmohammadi et al. used **F2**, which weights recall over precision, reflecting surveillance priorities. — [IEEE DSAA 2014](https://ieeexplore.ieee.org/document/7058109/)
- **Base rate:** ~1% of closing prices are manipulated, and only a small fraction are prosecuted (~300 unprosecuted per prosecuted case in the thesis estimate). "Negatives" in unlabelled data are heavily contaminated with positives, so measured precision against confirmed labels is a *lower bound*. — [Comerton-Forde & Putniņš 2014](https://econpapers.repec.org/RePEc:oup:revfin:v:18:y:2014:i:1:p:23-66.); [Putniņš thesis](https://core.ac.uk/download/41234083.pdf)
- La Morgia et al. evaluate **detection time** (30 min → 25 s), making speed or lead time an explicit metric. — [arXiv:2105.00733](https://arxiv.org/html/2105.00733v2)
- **UMA circularity:** UMA is produced by IDX surveillance and is meant to protect investors from manipulation risk. — [Bareksa](https://www.bareksa.com/berita/saham/2024-01-25/jangan-terjebak-saham-gorengan-ini-ciri-ciri-dan-cara-menghindarinya/amp). NSE-style surveillance flags are triggered by abnormal price and volume variations. — [NSE WP](https://nsearchives.nseindia.com/s3fs-public/inline-files/Effectiveness%20of%20additional%20surveillance%20measures_WorkingPaper.pdf). Exchange flags are therefore largely deterministic functions of price and volume anomalies.
- Surveillance placement itself changes subsequent returns and volume: CAR falls after STASM inclusion, and abnormal volume falls after ASM inclusion. Post-flag data is contaminated by the intervention. — [Chari & Inamdar 2024](https://ideas.repec.org/a/wsi/rpbfmp/v27y2024i01ns0219091524500048.html); [NSE white paper](https://nsearchives.nseindia.com/s3fs-public/inline-files/Effectiveness%20of%20additional%20surveillance%20measures_WhitePaper.pdf)
- **Purged / embargoed CV for financial time series** [prior knowledge, not verified in-session]: López de Prado (2018), *Advances in Financial Machine Learning*, Wiley, ch. 7, recommends purging training samples whose label horizon overlaps the test set and adding an embargo.

### Inferences
- **Protocol for the engineer:**
  1. **Point-in-time features only.** Use rolling windows ending at t-1 for baselines, as-of free float and market cap, and the price-limit regime in force on day t. Do not use future reversal in online scores.
  2. **Walk-forward:** expanding training window, retrain monthly or quarterly, test on the next period. With 13 positives, use **leave-one-event-out** (or leave-one-year-out) for any supervised component, purging and embargoing ±60 trading days around held-out windows.
  3. **Event-level hit:** a window counts as detected if the stock ranks in the top-k (e.g. k = 10, 20, 50 of ~900 stocks) on ≥1 day within [window start − L, window end]. Report recall@k for several k, and hits under alternative alignments.
  4. **Month-granularity windows:** treat the window as the whole month(s), ±1 month buffer for lead. Report hit/miss and "first alert date" relative to the month start. Don't compute day-level precision inside such windows. Use **multiple-instance** logic: the event is positive if *any* day is flagged.
  5. **Lead time:** days from first top-k alert to window start, or to the first UMA or suspension date. Detecting *before* UMA is the main value-add over exchange surveillance.
  6. **Alert budget and precision:** report alerts per day and precision@k against a *union* of weak labels (UMA, suspension, later OJK action), clearly labelled as weak precision. Also do manual review of a random sample of top-k alerts, which is the most honest precision estimate when positives are mostly unlabelled.
  7. **Base-rate framing:** with ~900 stocks and ~1% manipulated stock-days, a top-20 daily list has a ~2% random hit rate. Lift over random and over simple baselines (top-20 by |return| or by abnormal volume) is the key comparison.
- **Using UMA without circularity:**
  - Don't use UMA as the *only* target. Otherwise the model learns to reproduce IDX's price/volume trigger rules, and the evaluation rewards that.
  - Use UMA as (a) a *feature-free* auxiliary label in PU training with lower weight, (b) a **lead-time benchmark** (does our alert precede UMA?), and (c) a negative-control comparison: what does our detector find that UMA does not, and do those alerts later see suspension or OJK action?
  - Exclude post-UMA and post-suspension days from training negatives and from evaluation, or flag them, because of the intervention effects documented in India.
- **Baselines to always report:** raw |abnormal return| rank; abnormal-volume rank; a "UMA rule clone" (price change beyond the threshold implied by the regime). A complex model that does not beat these is not useful.

### Gaps
- No published study was found that evaluates a manipulation detector against month-granularity regulator windows. The multiple-instance and event-level protocol above is the researcher's recommendation, not a sourced standard.
- IDX's exact UMA trigger criteria are not published in detail, as far as found here. This limits how precisely circularity can be measured.

## 5. Handling price-limit (auto-rejection) regimes

### Takeaway
IDX daily limits (ARA upper / ARB lower) are tiered by price and have changed several times: asymmetric 7% ARB from March 2020, later normalised in stages, a change effective 8 April 2025, and **asymmetric 7% ARB reinstated from 19 October 2026 to 31 December 2026**. Returns are therefore censored at regime-dependent bounds. Features should (a) normalise returns by the limit in force that day, (b) include limit-hit indicators and streak counts, and (c) treat limit-hit days as censored when estimating volatility or abnormal-return baselines.

### Cited Findings
- ARB was asymmetric at 7% from **9 March 2020** (IDX Board Decision Kep-00023/BEI/03-2020) during the pandemic. — [Bisnis.com (Dec 2022)](https://market.bisnis.com/read/20221228/7/1612909/ini-perbedaan-auto-rejection-saham-selama-pandemi-dan-masa-normal); [Bisnis.com (Jun 2022)](https://market.bisnis.com/read/20220617/7/1544935/ini-alasan-bei-pakai-strategi-arb-asimetris-7-persen-selama-pandemi)
- Under "normal" rules, ARB could be up to 35% for Rp 50-200 stocks and up to 25% for Rp 200-5,000 stocks (symmetric with ARA). — [Bisnis.com (Dec 2022)](https://market.bisnis.com/read/20221228/7/1612909/ini-perbedaan-auto-rejection-saham-selama-pandemi-dan-masa-normal)
- An ARB rule change took effect **8 April 2025** (Kep-00003/BEI/04-2025), during the April 2025 market shock. Detailed figures were not in search snippets. Later reporting states that ARB had been **15%** for stocks above Rp 10 before October 2026. — [search summary of Bisnis/RMOL/Tirto coverage](https://tirto.id/bei-ubah-aturan-batas-auto-rejection-saham-hD5e); [Bisnis.com (Oct 2026)](https://market.bisnis.com/read/20261021/7/2014443/memahami-batas-arb-7-asimetris-alasan-bei-hingga-efek-bagi-investor)
- **New:** IDX reinstated **asymmetric 7% ARB effective 19 October 2026 through 31 December 2026**, to be re-evaluated. ARA remains tiered at 35% (Rp 11-200), 25% (>Rp 200-5,000) and 20% (>Rp 5,000). A symmetric ARB had been planned for 1 January 2027. — [Bisnis.com (19 Oct 2026)](https://market.bisnis.com/read/20261019/7/2013651/bei-pangkas-batas-auto-rejection-bawah-arb-jadi-asimetris-7); [RMOL](https://rmol.id/read/2026/10/19/725421/aturan-baru-bei-batas-arb-dipangkas-jadi-7-persen-hingga-akhir-tahun); [Investortrust](https://www.investortrust.id/market/119705/mulai-hari-ini-bei-tetapkan-batas-arb-saham-7)
- On 20 October 2026 IDX set ARB for Rp 11-14 stocks to **Rp 1** (tick-size floor). — [Bisnis.com (20 Oct 2026)](https://market.bisnis.com/read/20261020/7/2014004/bei-ubah-batas-arb-saham-kategori-rp11-sampai-rp14-menjadi-rp1)
- **Special Monitoring Board (Papan Pemantauan Khusus, notation X):** launched June 2023. Full periodic call auction (5 sessions a day) since 25 March 2024. Rule I-X has 11 fundamental and liquidity criteria, e.g. a 6-month average price below Rp 51, free-float non-compliance, low liquidity. Revisions proposed include removing criteria 6, 7 and 10. FCA stocks also have their own auto-rejection rules, with changes being prepared in July 2026. — [Katadata](https://katadata.co.id/amp/ekonopedia/istilah-ekonomi/665ea36145c5c/papan-pemantauan-khusus-definisi-kriteria-dan-54-emiten-terbarunya); [Bareksa](https://www.bareksa.com/berita/saham/2024-03-25/bei-implementasikan-papan-pemantauan-khusus-tahap-ii); [Kontan](https://investasi.kontan.co.id/news/bei-menyesuaikan-aturan-papan-pemantauan-khusus-full-call-auction-ini-bocorannya); [Bisnis.com (Jul 2026)](https://market.bisnis.com/read/20260706/7/1985764/bei-siapkan-aturan-baru-auto-rejection-untuk-saham-fca)

### Inferences
- **Correction to the project brief:** the brief says "ARB 7% in 2020-2023, 10-35% otherwise". Per the October 2026 reporting, ARB is again 7% asymmetric from 19 Oct 2026. The 2025 regime appears to be 15% flat (needs official confirmation). The regime table must be a dated lookup, `(date_from, date_to, price_band, board) → (ara_pct, arb_pct, min_tick_floor)`, sourced from IDX Kep- decisions, not hard-coded eras.
- **Feature handling:**
  - `r_norm = r / limit_up` if r > 0, else `r / limit_down`. This puts "hit ARA" at 1.0 in every regime.
  - Binary `hit_ara` / `hit_arb` (close ≥ limit price minus one tick, computed from the prior close, price band and tick table).
  - `ara_streak`, `n_ara_20d`, and `arb_after_ara_streak` (a dump signature).
  - Rolling volatility excluding limit days, or a censored (Tobit-style) estimator, so that a regime with a 7% ARB doesn't make downside volatility look artificially low.
  - A regime-change indicator, so the model doesn't confuse a rule change with a behavioural change. Robust z-score baselines should optionally reset or adjust across regime boundaries.
- Under asymmetric ARB (7% down vs 25-35% up), a dump must play out over several ARB days. Multi-day post-peak drawdown is a more robust "dump" feature than single-day returns.
- Notation-X / FCA stocks trade under a different mechanism (call auction). Their volume and frequency are not comparable to continuous-auction stocks, so they need a board indicator or a separate model.
- Tick-size floors (e.g. Rp 1 at very low prices) make percentage returns discrete and large for penny stocks. Use the price band as a stratifier.

### Gaps
- The exact ARA/ARB tables for each period (2020 phased normalisation, 2023 changes, April 2025 details) were not retrieved from primary IDX documents. The engineer should build the table from the IDX Kep- decrees on idx.co.id.
- No academic source was found on modelling censored returns specifically for manipulation detection under price limits.

## 6. Practical pitfalls

### Takeaway
The main pitfalls fall into a few groups: survivorship (delisted and suspended gorengan stocks disappear from current lists); unadjusted corporate actions that create fake returns (rights issues and stock splits are common in IDX small caps); thin trading and zero-volume days; IPO dynamics (themselves a documented pump-and-dump locus on IDX); intervention effects after UMA or suspension; and market-wide shocks (COVID March 2020; April 2025) that create cross-sectional false positives.

### Cited Findings
- IPO early trading on IDX shows pump-and-dump and flipping patterns. — [IPO event study](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/40be21b5983e57bea73a8562acfcab246755a37e)
- Market-wide regime shocks coincided with rule changes: ARB 7% from 9 March 2020 (COVID), an ARB change effective 8 April 2025, and ARB 7% again from 19 October 2026. — [Bisnis.com 2022](https://market.bisnis.com/read/20221228/7/1612909/ini-perbedaan-auto-rejection-saham-selama-pandemi-dan-masa-normal); [Tirto](https://tirto.id/bei-ubah-aturan-batas-auto-rejection-saham-hD5e); [Bisnis.com Oct 2026](https://market.bisnis.com/read/20261019/7/2013651/bei-pangkas-batas-auto-rejection-bawah-arb-jadi-asimetris-7)
- Surveillance placement alters subsequent returns and volume (intervention effect). — [Chari & Inamdar 2024](https://ideas.repec.org/a/wsi/rpbfmp/v27y2024i01ns0219091524500048.html)
- Manipulated stocks are disproportionately small, illiquid and poorly governed. This is the same population most affected by thin trading and delisting. — [Aggarwal & Wu 2006](https://econpapers.repec.org/RePEc:ucp:jnlbus:v:79:y:2006:i:4:p:1915-1954); [Huang & Cheng 2015](https://ideas.repec.org/a/kap/rqfnac/v44y2015i4p791-815.html)
- Special Monitoring Board stocks (low price, low liquidity, free-float issues) moved to call-auction trading in 2024. Their trading microstructure differs. — [Bareksa](https://www.bareksa.com/berita/saham/2024-03-25/bei-implementasikan-papan-pemantauan-khusus-tahap-ii)

### Inferences
- **Survivorship:** build the universe from point-in-time listings, including delisted tickers and those under long suspension. Many OJK and gorengan cases end in suspension or delisting, so dropping them removes the positives.
- **Corporate actions:** adjust OHLC and volume for splits, reverse splits, bonus shares and rights issues (with theoretical ex-rights price). Flag ex-dates and exclude them from return z-scores. Unadjusted rights issues will look like crashes; unadjusted reverse splits will look like pumps.
- **Thin trading:** zero-volume days make returns stale. Compute returns over the last traded price, add `days_since_last_trade`, and require a minimum number of trading days in the baseline window. Use robust (median/MAD) baselines. A sudden jump from near-zero to high volume is itself a strong signal, but z-scores blow up when MAD ≈ 0, so floor the MAD.
- **IPO effect:** exclude the first N (e.g. 20-60) trading days from baselines and use a peer (recent-IPO) baseline, or flag separately. Don't let IPO "pops" dominate alerts unless the IPO window is intentionally in scope.
- **Index rebalancing and inclusion** (LQ45, IDX80, MSCI/FTSE small-cap) produce legitimate abnormal volume and returns around effective dates. Add event-calendar flags.
- **Market-wide shocks:** use market-adjusted returns and cross-sectional ranks per day. Compute a market-stress indicator (e.g. the share of stocks hitting ARB) to down-weight or annotate alerts on crash or rebound days. Rebounds after shocks (e.g. after April 2025) can produce clusters of ARA hits that look like pumps.
- **Suspension and UMA intervention:** suspended days have no trades. Mark them explicitly and don't forward-fill prices into return features. The first day after suspension is often a large move, so flag it.
- **Label leakage:** OJK sanction announcements arrive years after the window. Use only information knowable at time t in features, and keep OJK labels purely for evaluation.

### Gaps
- No source specific to IDX corporate-action adjustment practices, or to the frequency of rights issues among gorengan stocks, was retrieved.
- No study quantifying false-positive inflation from index rebalances or market shocks in manipulation detectors was found.
